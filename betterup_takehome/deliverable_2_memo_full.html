This memo addresses three areas flagged ahead of TechCorp's mid-year BVR: the active-user count discrepancy between systems, the coach response-time concern raised through support tickets, and a forward-looking framework for scaling BVR analytics across our partner portfolio.

1. Data Discrepancy Analysis: 340 vs. 298 Active Users

Root Cause Assessment

The discrepancy is almost certainly definitional, not a data error. The two numbers measure different things:

Source Count Definition
TechCorp HR (internal) 340 "Engaged" = logged in or attended an orientation session
BetterUp Platform (Looker) 298 "Active" = completed at least one coaching session

The gap of ~42 users likely represents employees who logged in or attended orientation but have not yet booked a coaching session. This is consistent with the broader activation funnel we observe in the platform data:

Funnel Stage Users % of Licenses
Total licenses purchased 500 100%
Completed profile setup 387 77%
TechCorp "engaged" (login or orientation) 340 68%
Completed 1+ coaching session (Looker) 298 60%
Key insight: Both numbers are correct — they answer different questions. The 298 figure represents the strongest measure of program adoption, while TechCorp's 340 reflects broader awareness and intent to participate. This is actually a positive signal: there is a warm pipeline of 42 users who have already shown interest and need a nudge to book their first session.

Troubleshooting Steps & Questions for the Offshore Analyst

Before presenting this conclusion to TechCorp, I would validate with the following investigation:

  1. Confirm the Looker definition: Does "active user" require a completed session, or does a booked/scheduled session count? Are canceled sessions excluded?
  2. Pull a login-only segment: Can we export a list of users who logged in or completed onboarding but have zero completed sessions? Does that cohort size reconcile the ~42 gap?
  3. Check date-range alignment: Is TechCorp's 340 count cumulative since launch (Jan 2025), or a snapshot? Is the Looker data using the same time window?
  4. Verify data refresh timing: When was TechCorp's count pulled vs. the Looker export? A lag of even a few days could create variance.
  5. Request TechCorp's user list (if possible): A 1:1 match between their list and ours would definitively isolate the gap and identify any users present in one system but not the other.

Recommendation for TechCorp: Establish a shared "active user" definition documented in a brief data dictionary, agreed upon by both teams. This eliminates confusion for future reviews and builds trust in the reporting.

2. Support Ticket Assessment: Coach Response Time

What the Data Shows

At first glance, coach response time appears in the data twice — in support tickets and in the survey. On closer inspection, both signals point to an isolated concern, not a systemic issue:

Data Point Finding Interpretation
Support tickets (response time) 8 tickets, but 5 from the same user 4 unique users = 1.3% of active users
Total support volume 23 tickets / 90 days / 298 users Extremely low ticket rate overall
Escalated tickets 2 total (not necessarily response-time related) Minimal severity
Survey: "Coach responds promptly" 3.8/5.0 Lowest survey score, but still positive

Approach to Determine if This Is a Real Problem

To move from "likely isolated" to a confident assessment, I would gather:

  1. Platform response-time data: Pull actual coach message response times from the platform — median, mean, 90th percentile, and distribution. Compare against BetterUp's SLA and cross-partner benchmarks.
  2. Coach-level analysis: Do the 4 unique users filing tickets share the same coach? If yes, this is a coaching-match issue, not a platform issue. If spread across coaches, investigate further.
  3. The repeat-filer: The user who submitted 5 of 8 tickets warrants individual attention — potentially a coach reassignment or direct outreach from the program administrator to understand expectations.
  4. Survey cross-tabulation: Segment the 3.8/5.0 score by tenure (new users vs. established). New users may have different expectations that normalize over time.
Thresholds for escalation: I would flag coach response time as a systemic issue if any of the following are true: (a) >10% of active users report the concern via tickets or survey scores, (b) the survey score drops below 3.5/5.0, (c) actual platform response times exceed SLA for >15% of messages, or (d) the issue is distributed across 3+ coaches rather than concentrated on one.

Recommendation for TechCorp: Acknowledge the feedback transparently, share what the broader data shows (high satisfaction, low ticket volume), and outline the specific steps being taken — including individual follow-up with the high-frequency reporter and a coach-level response-time review.

3. Scalability Framework: Efficient BVR Analytics at Scale

With 20+ partners requiring similar analyses, the current approach of ad-hoc, analyst-driven BVR prep does not scale. Below is a three-part framework to increase efficiency while maintaining quality.

A. Triage Model: Offshore Team vs. Senior Analyst

Handled by Offshore/BPO Analysts Requires Senior Analyst
Standard data pulls (utilization, survey, tickets) Narrative building and executive storyline
Template population with partner-specific data Anomaly investigation and root-cause analysis
Initial data reconciliation checks (definition-based gaps) Stakeholder-facing presentations and role-plays
Routine QA on dashboards and data freshness New framework or process design
First-pass support ticket categorization and summary Escalation decisions and judgment calls on ambiguous data

Escalation protocol: The offshore team completes a standard BVR data package using the template (see below). They flag any metrics that fall outside defined thresholds (e.g., utilization below 40%, NPS below +20, support tickets above a per-user rate). Flagged items are routed to the senior analyst for investigation and narrative framing.

B. BVR Template & Playbook

A standardized BVR kit would include:

  • Slide template: Pre-built executive summary slide with placeholder sections for utilization, satisfaction, and renewal recommendation — auto-populated where possible.
  • Data checklist: A step-by-step list of data pulls required for every BVR (Looker utilization, survey results, support tickets, Salesforce contract details), with instructions for the offshore team.
  • Discrepancy playbook: Common data mismatches and their typical root causes (definitional differences, date-range misalignment, refresh lag), so the offshore team can resolve routine issues without escalation.
  • Threshold reference card: Pre-defined benchmarks for key metrics so that both the offshore team and the senior analyst have a shared understanding of what "good" looks like vs. what requires investigation.

C. Automation & AI Opportunities

Opportunity Approach Impact
Auto-populate BVR templates Script or workflow that pulls partner data from Looker/Salesforce and fills the slide template Eliminates 30-60 min of manual data entry per partner
Anomaly detection Automated alerts when key metrics (utilization, NPS, ticket volume) deviate from historical baselines or cross-partner benchmarks Proactive issue identification; reduces reactive firefighting
Data reconciliation checks Automated comparison of partner-reported counts vs. platform counts, flagging gaps above a set tolerance Catches discrepancies before they surface in a BVR meeting
Support ticket summarization AI-generated summary of ticket themes, frequency, and unique-user counts per partner Replaces manual ticket review; surfaces patterns faster
First-draft narrative generation AI drafts an initial BVR narrative from structured data; senior analyst reviews and refines Reduces narrative prep time by ~50%; analyst focuses on judgment and storytelling
Phased rollout: Start with template standardization and the data-pull checklist (immediate, no tooling required). Next, automate the template population and reconciliation checks. Finally, introduce AI-powered narrative drafting and anomaly detection as the process matures and validation workflows are in place.
